# Project Reflection: Going from Google Slides to Docs as Code

## Project Intro

I took a current training **slide deck** and corresponding Google Doc **presenter guide**
and migrated it to a single Markdown file. In my current job, I deal with a 
lot of content within Google Slides and Google Docs. One of my goals for this
Technical Communication Certificate is to learn how my team can
adopt a more efficient content strategy. This assignment is a great start to
begin understanding what that strategy might look like.

### Formatting Applied to the Document

I started with looking at my slides and writing the Markdown for headers,
paragraph, lists, images, and links. Correlating each of those elements was
the easy part as I could identify those  quickly and it was only
a matter of remembering the Markdown.

* **Slide title:** *migrated to headers*
* **Bullet lists:** *migrated to bullet lists*
* **Images and links:** *migrated to images and links*

### Slide Deck Dilemma

However, I quickly ran into the issue of: ***what verbiage should come over?***

For example, If I have a slide title called *"Let's Navigate the Menu!"*, 
does that come over into my documentation? It's not a **fact**, it's not
important to the *content*. Rather, it's a visual, presentation, training
facilitation purpose.

And I always come back to **content and presentation are separate**. That is
and is going to continue to be my biggest pain point but also what will 
help me make decisions.

In the end, I did **not** bring over the slide deck content that did not
seem to bring any **value** to describing the setting or feature.

### Presenter Guide Audience Dilemma

After I got the slide deck content migrated over to Markdown,
I turned to my presenter guide and immediately hit a confusing roadblock.

**Who is my audience?**
I have two audiences: the **slide deck** talks to *attendees* and the
**presenter guide** is geared towards my *colleagues*. 

It did not seem right so I changed the presenter guide verbiage tense to be talking directly
to the *end user* and added that content in paragraph format under each heading.

I am still unsure how I'm going to reconcile that issue as I try and figure out
how I can use what I learn with my team and our content. The tradeoff of removing speaker 
directions is a pretty big one.
